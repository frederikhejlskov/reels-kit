# Writing a short-form script

Adapted for this kit from the skill "short-form-script-studio" by Ekos Labs
(github.com/Ekos-Labs/skills, MIT licence, see `LICENSE.txt`). Changed here:
the length comes from the creator and their own pace, a hook may come from
the creator's own material, reference reels come from the creator or the
kit's `swipe.md`, the choices are made in one go unless the creator asks
to go step by step, a missing fact is asked for and never written in, and
links to other products are removed.

A script workflow that does two jobs:
- **The workflow** decides what goes in the video, in a fixed order, one decision at a time.
- **The principles** decide how each line is written, so every line makes the next one necessary.

The result is a script in the creator's own voice and language, at the length they asked for, built on a structure that has already worked for someone else, without copying their words.

Reference reels come from the creator, or from the reels the kit's research saved in `swipe.md`. Without either, use the patterns in [outlier-patterns.md](outlier-patterns.md) and say that no reel was modelled.

Example request: "I want a reel about how I got my first client from a cold DM. Here's my voice note about it."

## Before you start

Collect, and ask only for what is missing:
- **The raw story**, in the creator's own words: a voice note, a thread, notes or a monologue. Read all of it before writing anything. If part is cut off, ask for it. Never draft from fragments.
- **Who it is for:** one specific viewer, not "an audience".
- **Their voice:** their own spoken words: the voice note or notes they just gave, and any transcripts of their own videos. Use them for word choice and rhythm. Never ask for more samples. See [spoken-format.md](spoken-format.md).
- **Reference reels**, if they have them, or the reels in `swipe.md`.

## The workflow

Make the choices in stages 1 to 4 yourself, then write the script. Show the script first and the four choices below it, one line each, so the creator can change any of them.

When the creator asks to go step by step, run one stage per message: after each stage, stop and wait for their choice, with options of one line each.

1. **Hook.** If the creator gives their own hook, take it and go to stage 2. A hook from an idea list or a formula is a suggestion, not the creator's own. Otherwise give five hooks, one line each (or choose the strongest when making the choices yourself). Take them first from the creator's own material: their most surprising moment, number or line, moved to the front. A hook may also use the real opening structure of an outlier reel recorded in `swipe.md` with the creator's facts in the slots: then name that reel and the ratio `swipe.md` recorded for it.
2. **Bridge.** What sits between the hook and the story: one or two sentences that turn the hook's tension into a reason to stay. Give three or four options, one line each, from outlier patterns (reframe, cost admission plus promise, belief reversal, stakes). Reels under about 45 seconds may skip the bridge.
3. **Story.** The shape that carries the conflict to the payoff. Beats only, one line each, using the creator's material. One named conflict, never several. When going step by step, the creator confirms or reorders.
4. **Format and edit note.** Which format (B-roll voiceover, vertical talking head, letterboxed horizontal), one reference reel for the editor, and exactly how it is edited.
5. **Script.** Follow [spoken-format.md](spoken-format.md): the creator's words line by line, at the length set in rule 4, no labels, no stage directions. Then change the lines the creator names, and only those.
6. **Hand back.** The kit's own steps take it from here.

**What counts as an outlier:** a reel `swipe.md` recorded at 2x or more of its page's usual views. Use the numbers `swipe.md` holds; do not go back to Instagram while writing. Never invent a reel, a view count or a pattern.

**Pattern check.** Before showing any hook, bridge or story, check it against the six points at the end of [outlier-patterns.md](outlier-patterns.md), and say which polarity target it pushes against.

## How each line is written

Apply these silently. They are judgement, not a visible checklist. Full notes: [attention-principles.md](attention-principles.md).
- Write to one person, as if the reel was sent to them.
- The hook creates a real question in the viewer's head. It does not announce a topic.
- The viewer keeps asking two things: is this valuable, and how much time will it cost me? Make the value feel big and the time feel small.
- Give something concrete (an insight, a proof, a surprise) in the first 5 to 10 seconds.
- A reel is read one line at a time. Every line must make the next line necessary. Open the next loop before the current one closes, and pay every loop honestly.
- Replace summaries with things people can picture: a number, a name, a date, a place, a screenshot.
- Find the most likely reason to scroll ("this is an ad", "too complicated", "only works for people with money") and answer it where it appears.
- Cut any line that does not serve the hook's promise, even a good one.

## Rules for the script

1. **Every claim traces to the creator's words.** You may only add connectors (and, so, but, then). When a fact, a belief or a motive is missing, ask for it before drafting. Never write one in.
2. **Conflict lines come only from opinions the creator stated** or confirmed when asked ("be a specialist", "you need a degree"). At most two per reel. Never invent "people say X".
3. **Mistakes stay mistakes.** Say what they did wrong, then what changed. Do not turn them into tips.
4. **Fit the length before drafting.** Use the length the creator asked for; without one, 40 to 50 seconds. Count words at the creator's own pace: words divided by seconds in a voice note or one of their own videos. Without a measured pace, use 3 words a second. Choose the beats that fit and say what was left out. Never hand over a draft that runs long. If the story needs far more room, it is an article, not a reel.
5. If the creator would be faster saying it, offer that: they record a voice note, you cut it to length and connect it.

## After the script

Below the script, in a few lines: the four choices, what was left out to fit the length, and a real verdict (post it or not, and the one weakest point). Do not give a menu of options.

## Output

1. The clean script first: one spoken sentence per line, ready to paste into a teleprompter.
2. Below it, separately: the on-screen text and shot list, and the source reels when any were modelled.

Never copy another creator's lines. Model structure, not words. This method only writes: recording, editing and posting are other steps.
